How an AirPlay song reaches the Raumfeld speakers

The Pi offers one AirPlay 2 speaker for each Raumfeld room, named after the room. It turns the sound it receives into MP3 web streams and asks the Raumfeld system, over UPnP, to play them. Rooms played to from the same device are joined into one zone, so Raumfeld keeps them in sync. Keys pressed on a speaker travel back to the device that is playing.

Each room has its own FIFO. Raw samples in it are 16-bit stereo at 44.1 kHz, about 176 KB per second. The FIFO carries sound only; play, pause and the sender’s address arrive separately, through each instance’s metadata feed.

What each stage does

  1. mDNSEach shairport-sync instance announces its speaker through Avahi. That is why the rooms appear in AirPlay menus without any setup on the phone.
  2. AirPlay 2The sender connects, pairs and streams. A helper called nqptp keeps a shared clock with the sender, which AirPlay 2 needs for timing.
  3. FIFOEach instance writes decoded samples into its own FIFO under /run/raumfeld-airplay. Volume is left untouched here, so the samples are always full level.
  4. Host APIThe bridge asks the Raumfeld host, on port 47365, which rooms exist, and moves rooms until one zone holds exactly the rooms being played to. A zone is what Raumfeld plays in sync.
  5. UPnPEach zone has a virtual player. The bridge hands it the stream address with SetAVTransportURI and sends Play. These are SOAP calls over HTTP, the same ones the Wren app uses.
  6. HTTPThe host fetches the room’s stream, such as /stream/wohnzimmer.mp3, from the Pi like an internet radio station. The stream has no end; ffmpeg encodes the samples as they arrive.
  7. RaumfeldFrom the host onward the sound stays inside Raumfeld’s own system, which keeps the rooms in time with each other.

Rooms come and go

The bridge reads the room list from the Raumfeld host every 10 seconds and keeps one shairport-sync instance running for each room that has a device.

What happensWhat the host reportsWhat the bridge does
A speaker goes into standbyThe room stays listed, with its deviceNothing. The AirPlay speaker stays, and the room wakes when played to.
A speaker is unpluggedThe room is dropped from the list within about 10 secondsRemoves its AirPlay speaker after 60 seconds, so a short dropout doesn’t make it flicker. Other rooms keep playing.
A speaker is plugged back inThe room is listed againStarts its instance again, with the same identity.
A room is renamedThe same room under a new nameRestarts the instance under the new name, same identity.
The host stops answeringNothingRemoves all AirPlay speakers after 60 seconds, and brings them back when it answers.

A room’s identity is its name tag, its port and its AirPlay device ID. The bridge stores these per room in /var/lib/raumfeld-airplay/rooms.json, so senders recognise a room across restarts and renames. If an instance crashes, the bridge starts it again.

One room or both

Each shairport-sync instance reports the sender’s address through its metadata feed. The bridge uses it to decide which rooms belong together.

Selected in AirPlayRaumfeld zonesStream played
One roomThat room aloneThe room’s own stream
Both, from one deviceOne zone with both roomsThe stream of the room that was playing first; the other room’s sound is discarded
Both, from two devicesTwo zonesEach room its own stream

When a room joins or leaves, the bridge reshapes the zone and keeps the stream it was already playing where it can. Until a sender’s address is known, the bridge assumes both rooms share a sender.

Starting, pausing and recovery

  1. VolumeAirPlay reports volume from −30 dB (quiet) to 0 dB (full). The bridge maps that range to Raumfeld’s 0 to 70 and sets it on that speaker’s room, also inside a shared zone. The cap of 70 is a setting.
  2. StartThe metadata feed says when play begins or resumes. The bridge groups the rooms and starts the stream then, before the first sound arrives, and feeds silence until it does.
  3. PauseThe feed also says when the sender pauses or play ends. The bridge stops the zone at once, so the speakers don’t play out what they had buffered. New sound starts the stream afresh.
  4. Quick togglesOn a skip or seek the sender reports resume and pause in quick succession. A pause within 1.5 seconds of a resume keeps the stream open instead of restarting it twice.
  5. FallbacksIf a report goes missing: 0.4 seconds without samples also counts as a stop, and a session that begins but stays silent is stopped after 8 seconds.
  6. Not ours any moreThe bridge only stops the zone if it is still playing the bridge’s stream. If someone switched the speakers to another source meanwhile, it leaves them alone.
  7. Dropped or stalled streamIf the speakers stop playing the stream while sound keeps arriving, or fall more than 6 seconds behind in fetching it, the bridge sends SetAVTransportURI and Play again.
  8. Finding the hostThe bridge remembers the host’s address from last time. Only if that fails does it search the network with SSDP.

Keys on the speakers

While a zone plays, the bridge reads its play state and each room’s volume from the speakers twice a second. A change it didn’t make itself is a key press, and it passes that on to the sender through shairport-sync’s remote-control interface on D-Bus.

KeyWhat the bridge seesWhat it does
PauseThe zone reports PAUSED_PLAYBACKSends Pause to the sender. The sender’s pause then stops the stream as usual.
Play, while pausedThe speakers fetch the room’s stream on their ownSends Play to the sender and feeds silence until the sound arrives.
VolumeA room volume the sender didn’t setSets the sender’s AirPlay volume to match, so its slider follows.

The sender echoes a volume it was given. The bridge recognises that echo and doesn’t apply it again. For 2 seconds after a real slider move on the sender, speaker-side readings are ignored, so the two don’t fight.

Where the delay comes from

Expect a few seconds between tapping play and hearing sound. The speakers buffer part of the stream before they start, and a live stream can only fill that buffer in real time. Because of this, the Raumfeld rooms are in sync with each other but not with other AirPlay speakers. Pause is quick, since the bridge stops the speakers as soon as the sender reports it.

The steps between the sender and shairport-sync are a summary of AirPlay 2, not a full account of it. Everything from the FIFO onward is exactly what the bridge on the Pi does.
